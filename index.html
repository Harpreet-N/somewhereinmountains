Hello, I'm Jenish Maharjan

For as long as I can remember, my life has been deeply intertwined with the rugged peaks and quiet majesty of the mountains.

Trekking isn't just about reaching the highest pass or ticking a destination off a map. It's about the rhythm of the trail and the stories shared along the way.

As a freelance trekking guide in Nepal, I spend my days navigating these trails, completely immersed in the wild spirit of the Himalayas. My greatest passion lies in bridging worlds: helping travelers deeply connect with raw, untouched nature while fostering genuine, respectful exchanges with the resilient local communities who call these mountains home.

Whether we're sharing a cup of butter tea in a remote stone teahouse or watching the sunrise paint the peaks gold, my goal is to make your journey feel less like a tour and more like a meaningful adventure.

What comes with every trek

The walking is only half of it. These are the things I add so you can stay present, stay well, and go home with more than photos of mountains.

Visual storytelling & trail keepsakes

  • Cinematic trail stories

    Instead of rushed phone snapshots, I capture smooth, stabilized video reels and natural, high-resolution portraits of you against sweeping Himalayan backdrops — delivering an edited highlight film at the end of our journey so you can stay present in the moment.

  • Your personal Himalayan trail log

    Receive a custom digital memory book after your trek, mapping out our exact daily elevation gains, GPS routes, peak names you stood before, and candid moments from the trail.

Mountain wellness & high-altitude care

  • Daily altitude & vitals check-ins

    Trek with peace of mind through structured morning and evening wellness checks using a pulse oximeter and blood pressure monitor, tracking your oxygen levels (SpO₂) and resting heart rate so your body adapts safely.

  • The trail comfort pouch

    Small comforts make a big difference at 4,000 meters. I carry a dedicated trail kit stocked with warm ginger-lemon-honey packets, local masala tea, electrolytes, and blister care for our rest stops.

  • Sunrise mobility & acclimatization breathwork

    Start your mornings with 10-minute gentle trail stretches and end your days with guided breathing exercises on the teahouse deck to help your lungs adjust to the thin mountain air.

Soulful cultural & village immersion

  • Teahouse kitchen & hearth sessions

    Step behind the counter with our local Didis (sisters) and Amas (mothers) to learn the art of folding momos by hand, frying crispy Gurung bread, or cooking traditional wood-fired dal bhat.

  • Living heritage & unscripted conversations

    Sit quietly inside centuries-old monasteries during morning puja chants, share salted butter tea with yak herders, and meet local artisans — with me translating real human stories rather than reciting textbook facts.

  • Whispering woods & skyline lore

    Discover the hidden ecology of the Himalayas as we spot Nepal's iridescent national bird (the danphe), identify high-altitude medicinal herbs used by local healers, and trace the names of every jagged peak on the horizon.

Local insider concierge — before & after the trail

  • Street-smart gear & logistics prep

    Meet a day before departure in Thamel or Lakeside to inspect and rent genuine down gear at local prices, set up your Nepali SIM/eSIM, and exchange currency safely without tourist markups.

  • Hidden courtyard food walks & recovery

    Celebrate your return from the mountains with a guided culinary walk through the secret alleys, bahals (courtyards), and street-food stalls of historic Patan or Kathmandu — or unwind with a trusted Ayurvedic recovery massage.

When to come to Nepal

Autumn, October to November, brings the clearest Himalayan views. Spring, March to May, means rhododendrons and warm river water. Monsoon, June to August, closes the rivers and muddies the trails.

Leave the trail better than we found it

Tourism feeds whole villages in Nepal. How we travel decides who benefits.

  • Fair treatment for porters and crew

    Porters carry a fair load and get proper gear, beds and meals. Everyone on the team is paid properly, not left to depend on tips.

  • Local teahouses and family lodges

    We sleep and eat where the money stays in the village. No big chains, no middlemen.

  • Wildlife without exploitation

    In Chitwan we use jeeps, canoes and our own feet. No elephant rides, ever.
