Every trip is priced for your group. The price depends on the route, number of days, group size, season, permits and transport such as the Lukla flight. Send your plans and you get a written quote listing what's included.

When the itinerary and price suit you, you confirm in writing and pay the deposit described in the booking terms. Sending an inquiry is free and doesn't commit you to anything.

Good broken-in walking boots, layers for cold mornings, a warm jacket, a rain shell, a sleeping bag for higher teahouses, a headlamp and a water bottle. We meet the day before in Thamel or Lakeside to check your gear and rent anything missing at local prices.

On most routes, yes. Since April 2023, foreign trekkers in Nepal's national parks and conservation areas need a licensed guide, and the TIMS card is issued through a government-registered trekking agency. The Everest (Khumbu) region applies its own local rules. Rules change, so I check the current ones when we plan your trip.

It depends on the region. Annapurna treks need an ACAP permit, Langtang a national park permit, the Everest region a Sagarmatha National Park and local area permit, and Manaslu is a restricted area with a special permit. I arrange them for you through my partner agency.

On most treks we stay in teahouses and family lodges: simple rooms, shared bathrooms higher up, and meals cooked on the spot. North Annapurna Base Camp is a camping trek, and the 2-day Trishuli trip includes a night in riverside tents.

Itineraries go up slowly and include acclimatisation days. I check how you feel every morning and evening, and if symptoms appear we slow down, rest or go down. Nothing can rule out altitude sickness completely, so please read up on the signs, and tell me about any health conditions before the trip.

Yes, it's required. Your policy must cover trekking up to your highest altitude, helicopter rescue and evacuation, medical treatment and, if you raft, white-water rafting.
